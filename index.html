BodyFlow conditions navigation on robot morphology so that the same visual observation can lead to body-feasible actions across different embodiments.

Abstract

In complex real-world environments, existing visual navigation foundation models often suffer from action ambiguity due to their lack of explicit awareness of robot embodiment. Given the same visual observation, robots with different geometries and kinematic constraints may require substantially different safe and feasible trajectories. To address this issue, we propose BodyFlow, a morphology-conditioned continuous generative framework for cross-embodiment visual navigation. BodyFlow constructs a large-scale morphology-conditioned cross-embodiment visual navigation dataset and parameterizes future trajectories as control points of continuous Bézier curves, yielding a low-dimensional, smooth, and analytically tractable action space. Furthermore, BodyFlow uses Morphology-Conditioned Flow Matching to learn a continuous generative process from visual observations to trajectory distributions. Robot geometry and kinematic constraints are explicitly incorporated into the trajectory generation model as conditioning information. To improve trajectory safety, BodyFlow uses a morphology-conditioned safety evaluator together with differentiable guidance from a distance energy field to guide trajectory generation. The action flow field is further refined in simulation through reinforcement-learning-based residual fine-tuning, enabling closed-loop correction of generated trajectories. Experimental results show that BodyFlow enables a single policy to support diverse robot embodiments, including wheeled, quadruped, and humanoid robots, while achieving higher navigation success rates and stronger obstacle avoidance performance in zero-shot navigation.

Key Insight: From Body-Agnostic Actions to Body-Aware Flow

BodyFlow treats morphology as an explicit condition instead of an implicit deployment detail. This makes action prediction less ambiguous when multiple robots observe the same scene but require different motion envelopes.

  1. Visual observations provide scene context and obstacle layout.
  2. Body descriptors encode platform-specific constraints and feasible motion envelopes.
  3. Motion-flow prediction preserves trajectory continuity for real-time robot control.
