Abstract

In complex real-world environments, existing visual navigation foundation models often suffer from action ambiguity due to their limited modeling of robot embodiment. Given the same visual observation and goal condition, robots with different body dimensions and kinematic limits may require substantially different safe and feasible trajectories. To address this issue, we propose BodyFlow, a morphology-conditioned continuous generative framework for cross-embodiment visual navigation. BodyFlow explicitly incorporates robot morphology into trajectory generation and learns embodiment-adaptive trajectory distributions from large-scale cross-embodiment navigation data. It performs flow-based generation in a Bézier control-point space, where predicted control points are interpolated into executable waypoints to reduce the dimensionality of the generative action space and encourage smooth trajectories. To improve body-level safety, BodyFlow further models body-contour-aware trajectory clearance and uses differentiable safety guidance to steer generated trajectories away from morphology-dependent collision risks. The generated action flow is refined through closed-loop residual fine-tuning in simulation, enabling the policy to adapt to physical interaction feedback while preserving the pretrained visual-navigation prior. Experiments in both simulation and real-world environments show that BodyFlow enables a single policy to support diverse robot embodiments, including wheeled, quadruped, and humanoid robots, while improving obstacle avoidance under different morphology conditions.

Method

BodyFlow treats morphology as an explicit condition instead of an implicit deployment detail. This makes action prediction less ambiguous when multiple robots observe the same scene but require different motion envelopes.

  1. Visual observations provide scene context and obstacle layout.
  2. Body descriptors encode platform-specific constraints and feasible motion envelopes.
  3. Motion-flow prediction preserves trajectory continuity for real-time robot control.
